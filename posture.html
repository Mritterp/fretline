Classical guitar posture

Before you play 0 / 9

A thirty-second check to run at the start of each session, and again whenever something starts to ache.

Tension builds up slowly. Every few minutes, check your shoulders, jaw and thumb.
01

The chair

Use a firm, armless chair at a height that keeps your thighs about level. Sit on the front half of the seat, on your sit bones, not slumped against the backrest.

Keep your spine tall without stiffening it, and lean forward just a little from the hips.

02

Use a guitar support

Attach the support to the lower side of the guitar (most hold on with suction cups) and rest it on your left thigh. Keep both feet flat on the floor, about hip-width apart.

Adjust its height until the headstock reaches about eye level and the guitar stays still without your hands.

The support raises the guitar instead of your leg, so your hips stay level and your lower back isn't twisted, as it can be with the traditional footstool.

03

Angle the neck

Point the neck up at about 35–45°, so the headstock is roughly at eye level. Higher angles bring the left hand closer and keep the wrist straighter.

Keep the top close to vertical. If you need to see the fingerboard, turn your head a little. Don't tip the guitar's face up toward you.

04

Music stand & eyeline

Set the stand up after the guitar is in position, so the page goes where your eyes already are. Put it straight in front of you, about an arm's length away (roughly 60–70 cm / 2 ft), and raise it until the top of the page is about at eye level.

Read with your eyes, not your head: chin level, eyes drifting down the page. With the headstock and the page at similar heights, a glance at the fingerboard is a small eye movement, not a nod.

Reading Fretline on a phone, tablet or laptop? Put the screen on the stand (or prop it on books) at that same height and distance. A screen lying flat on a table pulls your head down over it.

05

Left hand

Put the pad of the thumb behind the neck, about opposite the second finger. Don't hook it over the top. Keep the wrist almost straight with a slight forward curve.

Curve the fingers and play on the tips, just behind the fret, pressing only as hard as a clean note needs. Let the elbow hang freely, neither pinned to your side nor sticking out.

Stay close to the fret wire. The nearer the fingertip is to it, the less pressure a clean note takes, so you avoid buzzing without squeezing. If a note buzzes, move closer to the fret before pressing harder, then ease off until it's only just clean.

06

Right hand

Rest the forearm, not the elbow, on the top edge of the lower bout. The hand then hangs over the strings between the soundhole and the bridge.

Line the wrist up with the forearm with a slight arch, and don't bend it sideways. Keep the thumb (p) out ahead of the fingers toward the neck, with i m a over the treble strings, each moving from its big knuckle.

07

Shoulders & breath

Keep both shoulders level and dropped. The right one tends to creep up as you reach over the guitar. Unclench your jaw and keep breathing through hard passages.

The test: let go with both hands. The guitar should stay where it is.

Common faults
  • Face tipped toward the ceilingThe guitar is lying back so you can watch your hands, which twists the left wrist.
  • Neck droopingThe headstock is lower than the shoulder, so the left arm has to reach and the wrist collapses.
  • Hunched right shoulderUsually the guitar is too far forward or the chair is too low. Fix the setup first, not the shoulder.
  • Thumb over the topFine for steel-string and electric. On classical it shortens your reach and pulls the fingers off vertical.
  • Support set too lowThe guitar sinks and you end up bending over it. Raise the support before you change anything else.
  • Leaning over the guitarBring the guitar up to you instead of bending down to it.
  • Music stand too lowYou nod down over the page, rounding your neck and shoulders. Raise the stand to your eyes instead of dropping your head to it.
  • Stand too far or too closeToo far and you lean in to read; too close and it crowds your arms. Aim for about an arm's length.
